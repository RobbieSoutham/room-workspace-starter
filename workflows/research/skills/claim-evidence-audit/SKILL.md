# Claim and evidence audit

Use when checking factual, numerical or interpretive claims in a manuscript, report, presentation or research decision. This is a traceability and support review; it does not certify the overall validity of a study.

## Workflow

1. Read the canonical [evidence and claims rules](../../knowledge/evidence-and-claims.md). Identify the document version and the claims in scope.
2. For each material claim, locate its cited source, passage, dataset or calculation. Check the source itself and enough context to interpret qualifiers, population, method and units.
3. Classify support as `SUPPORTED`, `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`, `NOT_SUPPORTED`, `CONTRADICTED` or `UNTRACEABLE`. Explain the reason and the narrowest wording the evidence can sustain.
4. For numbers, check the data and calculation route, not only a citation. For quotations, distinguish exact text matching from broader support for the claim.
5. Record uncertainty, missing sources, unresolved conflicts and review limitations. Do not infer that a source supports a claim merely because its topic is relevant.
6. Report a table of claim, evidence inspected, status, reason and proposed action. Verify bibliography details before calling the citation audit complete.

If the evidence is unavailable, state that the claim could not be assessed. Do not invent a source or treat a generated citation or summary as verification.
