# Research principles

**Owner:** Research room · **Status:** Canonical source of truth for shared research practice in this workspace template.

This document records the room's durable principles. It is not a discipline-specific method manual. Projects should use the standards and approvals that apply to their field, institution, funder, publisher and data.

## Question before method

Frame the problem as a question that evidence can answer. Record why the answer matters, its scope, plausible alternative explanations, and what evidence would support, weaken or leave the claim unresolved. Decide what a reader will inspect to judge the answer: for example, a figure, comparison table, proof, synthesis, dataset or demonstration.

The question determines what kind of evidence is needed. Do not let an available tool or dataset define the claim by convenience.

## Keep exploration distinct from confirmation

Exploratory work discovers possibilities and helps form questions. Confirmatory work evaluates a specified claim against a plan. Both are useful; label the work accurately. If exploration leads to a new confirmatory question, frame it separately rather than rewriting the earlier plan as if the new question had been set in advance.

For a new claim-bearing project, copy and complete [`../templates/research_frame.md`](../templates/research_frame.md) in the project repository. Existing frames are project records: update by version or log material changes; do not replace their history silently.

## Understand prior evidence and alternatives

Establish what is already known and how it was established before choosing a method. Compare methods, populations, measures, assumptions and limitations. Include relevant disagreement and evidence that challenges the preferred explanation. A review should explain how the evidence bears on the question, rather than only list source summaries.

## Match design to the claim

Choose the design by the kind of evidence the question needs. Make the unit of analysis and the key comparison, interpretation or proof obligation explicit.

| Research mode | Choices that should be inspectable |
|---|---|
| Quantitative or experimental | Units, comparison, outcomes, controls, exclusions, missingness and analysis plan |
| Qualitative or interpretive | Sampling, setting, researcher role, data generation, analytic approach and treatment of rival readings |
| Literature review | Sources, search date, scope, eligibility rules, screening and synthesis method |
| Computational or data science | Data lineage, preprocessing, baselines, splits, metrics, software/environment versions and failure cases |
| Theoretical or mathematical | Definitions, assumptions, scope, proof strategy and possible counterexamples |
| Design or software research | Intended use, requirements, evaluation criteria, test cases and limits on generalisation |

A working system establishes that specified behaviour occurred under specified conditions. It does not, by itself, establish a broader claim about effectiveness or the world.

## Bound conclusions to evidence

State claims at the scope and certainty the evidence supports. Make assumptions, uncertainty and limitations visible. Do not turn association into causation, a local demonstration into broad effectiveness, or absence of evidence into evidence of absence without a suitable design. Claim-to-source rules and review labels are defined in [`evidence-and-claims.md`](evidence-and-claims.md).

## Maintenance

This is the policy source for the Research room. Skills should link here rather than restate these principles. Change the document when shared practice changes, then check related skills and templates for alignment. Project-specific methods and decisions belong in the project repository.
