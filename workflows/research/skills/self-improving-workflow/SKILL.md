# Self-improving workflow

1. Identify a recurring failure or friction and collect examples, including failed attempts.
2. State the proposed change, expected benefit, risk and evidence that would disconfirm it.
3. Test the smallest revision against representative cases and preserve original outputs.
4. Compare error types and workload, not only success rate; obtain independent review for consequential changes.
5. Promote only if evidence supports the change. Record version, limits, rollback and review date.

Return a change record with before/after evidence and disposition. Do not turn a one-off preference into permanent policy without a recurrence or reasoned exception.
