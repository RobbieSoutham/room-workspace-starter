# Research communication

Use for a talk, abstract, poster, briefing or public-facing explanation based on existing research.

1. Identify the audience, purpose, format, length and what the audience should understand or decide.
2. Locate the canonical study results and claim-evidence record. Ask for missing sources rather than treating a draft summary as primary evidence.
3. Select the smallest set of claims that serves the purpose. Preserve scope, uncertainty, caveats and distinctions between results and implications.
4. Build a clear sequence suited to the audience; define necessary terms and explain why evidence supports each conclusion.
5. Check citations, numbers, figures and wording against the source material. Mark simplifications and unresolved facts for author review before circulation.
