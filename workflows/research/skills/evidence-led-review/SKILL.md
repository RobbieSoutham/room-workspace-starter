# Evidence-led review

Use for literature searches, scoping or systematic reviews, evidence maps and synthesis. For a narrow fact check or claim audit, use `claim-evidence-audit` instead.

## Workflow

1. Read the canonical [research principles](../../knowledge/research-principles.md) and [evidence and claims rules](../../knowledge/evidence-and-claims.md). Locate any existing project protocol and source ledger.
2. Define the review question, scope, intended synthesis and review type. Choose search sources that fit the question and the field.
3. Before screening when reproducibility matters, record sources, queries, search dates, limits, eligibility rules, deduplication and screening plan. Follow applicable review standards; do not label a review systematic unless its method warrants that label.
4. Keep an evidence record for each included source: stable reference, locator, relevant finding, method/context and limitations. Separate the source's finding from your interpretation.
5. Synthesize comparisons, patterns, disagreements and gaps across sources. Preserve evidence that challenges the emerging account and identify where the literature cannot support a firm conclusion.
6. Verify citations and identifiers against sources before circulation. State search limits and avoid claiming exhaustive coverage beyond the documented method.

When a project ledger is needed, copy [`../../templates/evidence_ledger.csv`](../../templates/evidence_ledger.csv) into the project repository. Keep PDFs, exports and restricted data in their approved storage.
