# Research question development

Use when a topic is too broad, multiple questions are entangled, or the intended contribution is unclear. For a complete project frame, use `research-frame`.

1. Identify the problem, intended audience and why an answer matters; distinguish these from assumptions about the answer.
2. Map the key concepts, population or cases, setting, time span and relevant outcomes or interpretive focus.
3. Draft a small set of candidate questions and test each for clarity, answerability, significance and feasible evidence.
4. State plausible competing answers and what observation or interpretation could change the conclusion.
5. Recommend a question with explicit inclusion and exclusion boundaries. Record unresolved scope decisions; do not imply novelty without a literature check.
