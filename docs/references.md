# Methods and reporting references

These sources inform the workflow defaults. A guideline's scope matters: reporting guidance does not by itself establish that the method was appropriate or correctly performed.

- Page MJ et al. [The PRISMA 2020 statement: an updated guideline for reporting systematic reviews](https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71), *BMJ* 2021;372:n71. Use for transparent reporting of systematic reviews; consult the explanation and elaboration paper and relevant extensions for the review type.
- Rethlefsen ML et al. [PRISMA-S: an extension to the PRISMA Statement for Reporting Literature Searches in Systematic Reviews](https://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13643-020-01542-z), *Systematic Reviews* 2021;10:39. Search reporting guidance; it is not a conduct or search-quality appraisal tool.
- Lefebvre C et al. [Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 4: Searching for and selecting studies](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04), version 6.5.1, updated March 2025. Practical methods guidance for systematic reviews of interventions; its scope is not every field or review design.
- Wilkinson MD et al. [The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18), *Scientific Data* 2016;3:160018. A framework for findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable data; FAIR does not mean public or unrestricted.
- Clark J et al. [Generative artificial intelligence use in evidence synthesis: a systematic review](https://doi.org/10.1017/rsm.2025.16), *Research Synthesis Methods* 2025;16(4):601–619. Reports substantial variation and error across evidence-synthesis tasks and concludes that current evidence does not support unsupervised use. Treat AI as an aid with explicit human oversight and task-specific validation.
- EQUATOR Network. [PRISMA-AIM: AI use in systematic reviews](https://equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/under-development/prisma-aim/), project registered 29 October 2026 and marked under development. It may inform future reporting; it is not yet a completed checklist.

Check official sources for updates before using a reporting checklist or venue rule. The workflow is evidence-informed, not certified as state of the art or as a substitute for field-specific methods expertise.
