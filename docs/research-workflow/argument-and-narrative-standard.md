# Argument and narrative standard

Build a defensible argument rather than a dramatic story or source-by-source summary. For expert work, make the sequence answer: what is established, what remains unresolved, why it matters, what question follows, what evidence could answer it, and what would change the conclusion?

## Pre-result argument map

Write alongside the protocol, before outcome inspection:

1. **Territory:** what the literature supports, grouped by mechanisms or questions.
2. **Tension:** the contradiction, boundary, failure mode or missing link.
3. **Consequence:** what cannot yet be decided, explained or trusted.
4. **Question and falsifier:** what is being asked and what evidence would weaken the preferred explanation.
5. **Conditional contribution:** what the work may add if the test succeeds.
6. **Method warrant:** why the proposed evidence can answer the question.
7. **Closest alternative:** strongest competing explanation or related work.
8. **Gap maturity:** verified, provisional, speculative or closed, with search evidence.
9. **Decision:** proceed, narrow, gather evidence or stop.

Version the map. Do not rewrite the pre-result rationale after seeing results; create a post-result map and explain material differences.

## Evidence classes

Label material statements as literature-supported, locally observed, pilot-only, inference, hypothesis, planned contribution, unknown, contradicted or withdrawn. Proposals may make conditional arguments from literature and inference; they must not present planned results as observed evidence.

## Gap discipline

Avoid “no one has done X” without a documented search and closure check. Name the gap more precisely: contradiction, mechanism, evaluation, boundary, translation, reproducibility or negative evidence. A limitation in one local method is not automatically a field-wide gap.

## Post-result claim map

| Claim | Evidence | Warrant | Scope/qualifier | Alternative or rebuttal | Check state |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Compare it with the pre-result map. State which prediction survived, whether the study answered the original question, what assumptions changed, and whether the contribution narrowed, changed, became null or was withdrawn. Keep ambiguous and negative results when the design and data support reporting them.

Story elements can clarify sequence and stakes, especially for non-specialists, but must not manufacture inevitability or conceal uncertainty. Adapt wording and detail to audience while preserving the same evidence boundary.
